Our Family Story

Before I began photographing dogs in Calgary, I was already a pet parent.

Our home is shared with two dogs, two cats, and the lasting love of a Chow Chow we had to say goodbye to. I know the little things are never little: the ears that rise when you say one word, the place they wait for you, and the expression that only their person recognizes. This is where the heart behind Dog Stories by Max begins.

A day worth remembering · Lake Louise

The view was beautiful. Watching them enjoy it was better.

At Lake Louise, Buddy and Charlie became part of the scenery in their own way. Visitors stopped to smile and ask for photos with them. Buddy stood quietly taking in the mountains and turquoise water, almost as if he understood exactly why everyone had come. Charlie stayed true to himself — independent, unhurried and perfectly happy to take a short nap beside one of Canada’s most famous views.

Days like this remind us not to wait for the perfect time. While they can still walk, wander and discover, we make room to bring them with us — to new trails, new views and new little adventures. The destination matters, but the real gift is watching them explore, rest and simply enjoy being together.

Our family

Our everyday crew.

Meet the personalities, routines and funny little habits of the two dogs and two cats who make our home feel full.

Leung Fun & Sai Mai Lo

The first big brothers of our family.

Long before Buddy, ChuChu and Charlie came into our lives, Leung Fun and Sai Mai Lo were already here. Now sixteen, they are the true senior brothers of our home — smaller in size, perhaps, but certainly not in status. The dogs have always seemed to understand this too, treating them with the quiet respect reserved for the eldest members of the family.

Leung Fun is the affectionate one. When the house begins to settle for the night, he lets us know it is time to hold him close. He loves falling asleep in our arms and is just as welcoming when friends or family visit — always happy to come forward for a gentle pat.

Sai Mai Lo is his complete opposite: independent, observant and very much on his own schedule. He does not usually ask for attention, but when the mood is right, he will jump onto a lap and quietly ask to be loved.

They are easy to tell apart: Sai Mai Lo has the more leopard-like tabby markings, while Leung Fun has his own softer, unmistakable look. We hope these two first members of our family will stay healthy, comfortable and happy for many more years.

Buddy

From little brother to big brother.

Buddy has always loved being around people and other dogs. He is the kind of dog who wants to be part of every hello, every walk and every little moment of fun.

When ChuChu was still with us, Buddy was the little brother: always looking for a game, a reaction or simply a moment close to him. When dinner was being prepared, Buddy would sit patiently in the kitchen — although he was never against checking whether ChuChu might share a little extra.

Buddy and ChuChu arrived home on the same day and grew up side by side. After ChuChu passed away, Buddy would smell the harness his brother used to wear and cry. Losing the companion he had known every day of his life brought him deep separation anxiety.

That is why, while Buddy was still only two, we brought Charlie into our family. In a short time, Buddy went from being the little brother to becoming a big brother — and found companionship again.

Charlie

The little brother who helped Buddy heal.

In 2018, after we said goodbye to ChuChu, we quickly realized how deeply Buddy needed a companion. Then, as if the timing had been quietly arranged for us, Charlie came home.

Charlie helped heal a part of Buddy’s heart. Although Charlie and ChuChu are both Chow Chows, they could not be more different. As a puppy, Charlie was full of energy and loved being close to us. He always wanted cuddles, and even when Buddy was trying to sleep, Charlie would keep bothering his big brother to play.

Buddy was the older brother, but he was only two years old himself. When Charlie became a little too much, Buddy would give him a gentle correction — the kind only an older brother can give. To us, those playful little moments were always full of warmth.

As Charlie grew up, he became quieter and more independent. Unlike Buddy, who loves greeting every person and dog he meets, Charlie often prefers to explore at his own pace.

Before our move to Calgary, Charlie unfortunately injured his back leg. So the photographs here of him running, leaping and playing freely mean even more to us now. We look forward to the day he is healed, comfortable, and able to run and jump again.

That is part of why this page matters to me. Our dogs do not stay in one chapter of life forever. The playful days, the quiet days, the ordinary walks and the small ways they make us laugh — they become priceless without us even realizing it. I hope to help other families hold onto those moments, too.
