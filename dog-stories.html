Surprise prints · real beginnings

Sometimes the story begins before we even know their name.

Sometimes it begins with a dog I notice in passing. Sometimes it begins at a front door, when someone has trusted me to care for a dog already at the centre of their life.

These moments come from strangers I meet and dogs I get to know through Rover. There may never be a formal portrait session, and that is never the point. I simply notice something true about them, make a photograph, and leave their person with a small print — something real they can hold, and a happy memory of the dog they love.

The first surprises

A small print. A real reaction.

No booking, no posing, no long introduction. Just a dog being completely themselves, and a photograph their person did not expect to receive.

Wally · A first Rover visit in Calgary

He greeted me like we already knew each other.

Some dogs need time to decide whether you belong. Wally only needed to hear his name.

This was my first Rover booking in Calgary. I had barely stepped through the front door when the five-year-old Goldendoodle ran over, took in my scent and kissed my face as though we were already friends. After his family left, I simply stayed with him. We played until he forgot about the camera and became completely himself — calm and attentive outside, then joyfully determined with his blue-and-green rope.

I had planned to leave one photograph. Then I saw the way he sat on our walk. Then that face around the rope. One print quietly became three.

When his family returned, I told them I had a surprise. His person looked at the photographs and asked, “How much?” I told them the three prints were free. I only wanted the family to have a happy memory of an ordinary afternoon with the dog they love.

I hope Wally keeps greeting the world exactly as he greeted me: with his whole heart.

Surprise No. 01 · A winter afternoon

Before the moment disappeared.

A black dog leapt through a snowy afternoon while their person stood nearby in a bright pink hat. I made the photograph, edited it from the car, printed it, and walked back across the ice. The picture was small. Her smile was not.

Surprise No. 02 · Full speed

He came for a walk. He left with a memory.

A dog came charging across the frozen field, full of the kind of energy that never waits for a camera. A few minutes later, that movement became a print — handed to the young person who knows that face better than anyone.

Surprise No. 03 · The favourite stick

The joy you cannot ask a dog to repeat.

A stick, a stretch of snow, and a dog running as if nothing else in the world mattered. I caught the moment, made the print nearby, and returned it to the person who had been watching that joy all along.
